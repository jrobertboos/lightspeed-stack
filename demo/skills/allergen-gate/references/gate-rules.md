# Allergen gate rules

These verdicts exist only in this skill. Do not substitute FDA, EU, or restaurant-chain allergen charts.

## Gate-9 allergens

milk, egg, peanut, tree nut, wheat, soy, fish, sesame, lupin

If the guest names an allergen not on Gate-9, still treat it as a gate allergen (HOLD at minimum).

## Verdicts

| Verdict | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| CLEAR | The allergen is not in any dish **and** no shared surface, oil, or utensil touched it. |
| HOLD | The plated dish can be made safe, but only with a **new** pan, board, oil, and utensils, cooked **before** any allergen dish. |
| STOP | The menu as described cannot be made safe. Skipping, scraping, or "I'll just leave it off their plate" is not enough. |

## Cross-contact rules

- Shared fryer oil is STOP for that allergen, even if this guest's food has none of it.
- Sesame slaw, sauce, or garnish prepped in the same kitchen as the guest's food is STOP unless that item is not cooked at all this meal **and** its tools never touch the guest's food.
- "Leave it off the plate" or "scrape it off" is always STOP. The allergen already touched the serving path.
- A dedicated pan + fresh oil + washed board, cooked first, with the allergen dish cooked after the guest is served, is HOLD — never CLEAR.

## Next steps

- **CLEAR**: Serve as planned. Note the allergen on the ticket.
- **HOLD**: Cook the guest's plate first on a clean pan with fresh oil. Do not reuse tongs. Serve them before the allergen dish hits the stove.
- **STOP**: Do not serve this menu. Drop the allergen dish entirely (do not cook it this meal) **or** choose a different meal. Do not offer "we'll be careful."

## Worked example

Menu: fried chicken + sesame slaw. Guest allergic to sesame. Plan: leave the slaw off their plate.

- Sesame is Gate-9.
- Slaw is in the kitchen; tongs and boards are shared; skipping the plate does not remove cross-contact.
- Verdict: STOP. Fix: do not make sesame slaw this meal; fry the chicken in fresh oil on a clean pan.
